[Title] Waste Pickup & Recycling Platform: System Specification and Design
[Subtitle] Working name: EcoCollect (a Yo-Waste-style on-demand waste management platform). Version 1.0 draft
[Heading 1] 1. Introduction
[Heading 2] 1.1 Purpose
[First Paragraph] This document specifies the requirements and technical design for a two-sided mobile platform that connects households and businesses with waste haulers. It supports on-demand and scheduled pickups, sorted-waste diversion to recycling hubs, cashless mobile money payments, and extended services such as bin provisioning, consulting, and sanitation or water delivery.
[Heading 2] 1.2 Scope
[First Paragraph] In scope: Customer App, Hauler App, Admin and Dispatch Console, Recycling Hub Portal, backend services, payment integration, notifications, analytics. Out of scope for v1: municipal landfill weighbridge integration, carbon-credit trading, hardware IoT bin sensors (listed in the roadmap).

[Heading 1] 2. Overall Description
[Heading 2] 2.1 Product perspective
[First Paragraph] A cloud-hosted platform with two native-feeling mobile apps (Customer and Hauler), a web-based Admin Console and Hub Portal, and a shared backend exposed through a REST API with real-time channels.
[Heading 2] 2.2 User classes
[Compact] Customer (household or business): requests pickups, manages subscription, pays, rates service.
[Compact] Hauler/Driver: accepts or is assigned jobs, navigates, confirms collection.
[Compact] Fleet Manager: manages drivers and vehicles for a hauler company.
[Compact] Hub Operator: confirms receipt of sorted waste, records weights.
[Compact] Dispatcher/Admin: monitors operations, resolves exceptions, configures pricing.
[Heading 2] 2.3 Operating environment and constraints
[Compact] Android first (dominant in target markets), iOS second; Android 8+ and low-end devices with 2 GB RAM.
[Compact] Intermittent connectivity: apps must be offline-tolerant (queue actions, cache map tiles and routes).
[Compact] Data-light design: compressed payloads, small images, SMS/USSD fallback for key actions.
[Compact] Payments restricted to licensed mobile money aggregators and bank APIs.
[Heading 2] 2.4 Assumptions and dependencies
[Compact] A mobile money aggregator or direct provider APIs are available (with sandbox access).
[Compact] Google Maps Platform or an OpenStreetMap-based provider for maps, geocoding, routing.
[Compact] Households can describe location by pin drop, since formal addresses may be absent.

[Heading 2] 3.5 Core business rules
[Compact] A pickup is dispatched only to haulers in the customerΓÇÖs service zone with capacity and a valid vehicle.
[Compact] Sorted streams (plastics, paper, organics, glass, metal) are routed to the nearest accepting hub; general waste goes to the designated landfill or transfer station.
[Compact] Subscriptions generate pickups automatically at the start of each scheduling window; unpaid subscriptions enter a configurable grace period (e.g.┬á7 days) before suspension.
[Compact] On-demand pickups require payment authorisation or wallet balance before dispatch, or post-pay for trusted accounts.
[Compact] Hauler payouts are computed per completed job and released on a configurable cycle after customer confirmation or an auto-confirm timeout.
[Compact] A pickup missed through hauler fault triggers re-dispatch within a set SLA and a service credit to the customer.

[Heading 1] 6. Detailed Design
[Heading 2] 6.1 Pickup state machine
[First Paragraph] REQUESTED ΓåÆ PAYMENT_PENDING (if prepaid) ΓåÆ SCHEDULED / SEARCHING ΓåÆ ASSIGNED ΓåÆ EN_ROUTE ΓåÆ ARRIVED ΓåÆ COLLECTED ΓåÆ DELIVERED_TO_HUB ΓåÆ COMPLETED
[Body Text] Exceptional states: CANCELLED, SKIPPED (access problem, bin not out), FAILED (hauler issue, triggers re-dispatch), DISPUTED.
[Body Text] Every transition is an event stored in an append-only history table, which drives notifications, tracking, and audit.
[Heading 2] 6.2 Dispatch and route optimisation
[First Paragraph] On-demand matching
[Compact] Find online haulers within a radius using a Redis geo index (GEOSEARCH).
[Compact] Filter by zone, vehicle capacity, accepted streams, and current load.
[Compact] Score candidates: score = w1 * ETA + w2 * detour_cost + w3 * rating + w4 * fairness.
[Compact] Offer the job to the top candidate for 30 s; on decline or timeout, offer to the next. After N attempts, escalate to the dispatcher.
[First Paragraph] Scheduled route planning (nightly batch)
[Compact] Collect next-day pickups per zone and stream.
[Compact] Solve a Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem with Time Windows (CVRPTW) using OR-Tools or a routing engine, with constraints: vehicle capacity, shift length, customer time windows, and hub drop-off trips when full.
[Compact] Publish routes to the Hauler App by early morning.
[Compact] During the day, re-optimise incrementally when on-demand jobs, cancellations, or traffic changes occur.
[Heading 2] 6.3 Waste sorting and diversion flow
[Compact] Customer selects streams when scheduling; sorted streams may be collected on dedicated days or in compartmentalised vehicles.
[Compact] Hauler marks each stream collected at the stop (optional weight).
[Compact] System assigns each sorted load to the nearest hub that accepts the stream and has capacity.
[Compact] Hauler is routed to the hub; hub operator confirms receipt and records weight and contamination.
[Compact] Diversion rate = recycled and composted weight ├╖ total collected weight, reported per customer, zone, and period.
[Compact] Optional incentives: discounts or credits for high-quality sorting; notices for repeated contamination.
[Heading 2] 6.4 Payment design
[First Paragraph] Customer collections
[Compact] App creates an invoice or payment intent in PENDING status.
[Compact] Backend calls the mobile money providerΓÇÖs collection API (push prompt to the customerΓÇÖs phone) with a unique idempotency reference.
[Compact] Provider confirms asynchronously via webhook; backend verifies the signature, updates payment to SUCCESS or FAILED, and emits an event.
[Compact] A reconciliation job queries pending payments and compares provider statements daily.
[First Paragraph] Hauler payouts
[Compact] Earnings accrue per completed job in an internal ledger; payouts are disbursed to the driverΓÇÖs mobile money wallet via the providerΓÇÖs disbursement API on a schedule or on request, subject to a minimum.
[First Paragraph] Principles
[Compact] Double-entry ledger for all money movements (customer wallet, platform revenue, hauler payable, hub payable).
[Compact] All payment endpoints are idempotent; provider adapters are pluggable so new operators can be added without core changes.
[Compact] The platform never stores PINs; only tokenised phone numbers and provider references.
[Heading 2] 6.5 Real-time tracking
[Compact] Hauler App sends GPS points every 5 to 15 s while on shift (adaptive to speed and battery) over WebSocket or MQTT, batching points when offline.
[Compact] Location Ingest service writes the latest position to Redis and sampled history to PostGIS for audit and analytics.
[Compact] Customer App subscribes to its pickup channel to receive position and ETA, so only the assigned haulerΓÇÖs location is visible, and only during an active job.
[Heading 2] 6.6 Notifications
[First Paragraph] Event-driven: pickup assigned, hauler en route, arrived, completed, payment received or failed, invoice ready, schedule reminders. Channel order: push, then SMS fallback for critical events when the user is offline or has no data.
[Heading 1] 7. Data Design
[Heading 2] 7.1 Core entities
[Heading 2] 7.2 Key relationships
[Compact] A Customer has many ServiceLocations; a ServiceLocation has at most one active Subscription.
[Compact] A Subscription generates many Pickups; a Pickup has many PickupItems and PickupEvents.
[Compact] A Route has many RouteStops, each referencing a Pickup or a Hub drop-off.
[Compact] Payments belong to Invoices; every money movement creates balanced LedgerEntries.
[Heading 2] 7.3 Data storage notes
[Compact] PostGIS indexes (GiST) on locations and zone polygons; point-in-polygon for automatic zone assignment.
[Compact] Partition PickupEvent and location history tables by month.
[Compact] Archive data older than the retention period to cold storage; keep financial records per legal requirements.
[Heading 1] 8. API Design
[First Paragraph] REST over HTTPS with JSON, versioned under /api/v1, JWT bearer authentication, pagination, and idempotency keys on write operations that move money or change job state.
[Body Text] Real-time channels: pickup.{id} (status, hauler position, ETA), hauler.{id} (job offers, route changes), admin.zone.{id} (operational feed).
[Body Text] Sample request: create on-demand pickup
[Source Code] POST /api/v1/pickups
{
  "location_id": "loc_123",
  "type": "on_demand",
  "items": [{"stream": "plastics", "est_volume": "2_bags"}],
  "notes": "Bins behind the gate",
  "payment_method": "momo"
}
[Heading 1] 9. Security, Privacy, and Compliance
[Compact] Authentication: OTP with rate limiting and device binding; short-lived JWT plus refresh tokens; admin accounts require MFA.
[Compact] Authorisation: role-based and ownership checks on every resource (customers see only their data; haulers see only assigned jobs).
[Compact] Data protection: TLS everywhere, AES-256 at rest, field-level encryption for payout accounts, secrets in a managed vault.
[Compact] Privacy: collect precise location only when needed; hauler tracking limited to shifts; customer phone numbers masked in hauler-customer calls.
[Compact] Payment security: signed webhooks, idempotency, reconciliation, no storage of PINs or card data.
[Compact] Application security: input validation, rate limiting, WAF, dependency scanning, regular penetration tests.
[Compact] Fraud controls: fake pickups, duplicate payouts, GPS spoofing detection (speed and jump checks), photo and QR proof.
[Compact] Compliance: follow national data protection and e-transactions laws, environmental and waste handling regulations, and mobile money provider requirements.
[Compact] Backups and DR: daily full and continuous WAL backups; RPO 15 minutes, RTO 4 hours.
[Heading 1] 10. Deployment and Operations
[Heading 2] 10.1 Environments
[First Paragraph] Development, Staging (sandbox payment and map keys), Production. Infrastructure managed as code; blue-green or rolling deployments with automated rollback.
[Heading 2] 10.2 Infrastructure (initial)
[Compact] Managed Kubernetes or container service (2 to 3 nodes) with autoscaling.
[Compact] Managed PostgreSQL with read replica; managed Redis; managed broker.
[Compact] CDN for static assets; object storage for media.
[Compact] Region selection close to users to reduce latency, with a secondary region for backups.
[Heading 2] 10.3 Monitoring and alerting
[First Paragraph] Dashboards for API latency and errors, dispatch time-to-assign, payment success rate, queue depth, and app crash rate. Alerts page the on-call engineer for payment failures above threshold, dispatch stalls, and database health.
[Heading 2] 10.4 Operational processes
[First Paragraph] Hauler onboarding and training, customer support workflow (missed pickup within SLA), hub partnership agreements, and monthly service-quality reviews.
